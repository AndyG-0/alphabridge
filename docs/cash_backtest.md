# Cash-deployment backtest

_Generated by `services/cash_backtest.render_markdown()`; `tests/test_cash_backtest.py` fails if this file is stale._

Source: Damodaran annual returns (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/datasets/histretSP.xls), via `data/historical_returns.json`.

## Method

- 87 overlapping 10-year windows, starting 1930–2016. Each starts with 1.00 in cash.
- Cash earns the T-bill return; deployed money earns the S&P 500 total return and is never sold (as in the live engine).
- **Signal**: each January the rule deploys a share of the remaining cash chosen by a *reduced* score — 50% real cash yield (bills minus inflation), 50% prior-year stock return as a stand-in for 'below the 200-day average' — using the live engine's thresholds (HOLD under 45, DCA 45–70, aggressive above 70) and `cash_optimizer.deploy_fraction`.
- **Naive DCA**: deploy 25% of remaining cash every year regardless of signals.
- Compared with buying stocks on day one and with staying in bills. Multiples are ending value per 1.00 invested, pre-tax and pre-fee.

## Results

| Strategy | Median | Mean | Worst | Best | Beats lump sum | Beats cash | Loses money |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deployment rule (reduced score) | 2.11x | 2.37x | 1.20x | 4.93x | 32% | 80% | 0% |
| Naive DCA (fixed share of remaining cash each year) | 2.38x | 2.54x | 0.95x | 4.59x | 20% | 90% | 1% |
| Buy-and-hold stocks from day one | 2.88x | 3.06x | 0.87x | 6.25x | 0% | 87% | 3% |
| Stay in T-bills | 1.34x | 1.46x | 1.02x | 2.42x | 13% | 0% | 0% |

## What this does and does not show

- It cannot validate the live score: VIX, the earnings yield and the 200-day average are not in annual data, so the ERP and fear components (70% of the live weight) are absent.
- Annual steps hide intra-year timing; the prior-year return is a coarse drawdown proxy.
- Overlapping windows are not independent samples, so percentages overstate how much evidence there is.
- A deployment rule trades some expected return for lower regret and smaller drawdowns; read 'Worst' and 'Loses money' alongside the medians.
- Nothing here is a forecast; it is the history of one rule on one index.
